How many cubic feet in a cubic yard?

One cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. Picture a cube 3 feet long, 3 feet wide, and 3 feet tall — that is one cubic yard. The same yard covers 81 square feet at 4 inches thick.

The short answer: 27 cubic feet

A cubic yard is a cube that measures 3 feet on each side. Multiply the three sides: 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cubic feet. To change cubic yards into cubic feet, multiply by 27. To change cubic feet into cubic yards, divide by 27. The cubic yard converter does this math for you.

How many square feet does one cubic yard cover?

It depends on how thick the concrete is. Thinner pours cover more ground; thicker pours cover less. The rule is simple: square feet = 27 ÷ thickness in feet.

ThicknessSquare feet per cubic yard
2 inches162
3 inches108
4 inches81
6 inches54
8 inches40.5
12 inches27

Check the 4-inch row: 4 inches is one-third of a foot, so 27 ÷ 1/3 = 81 square feet. Check the 8-inch row: 8 inches is two-thirds of a foot, so 27 ÷ 2/3 = 40.5 square feet. Doubling the thickness cuts the coverage in half.

Cubic yards and cubic meters

One cubic yard is 0.765 cubic meters. Going the other way, one cubic meter is 35.31 cubic feet, which is about 1.31 cubic yards. If a plan or a supplier talks in cubic meters, multiply cubic meters by 1.31 to get cubic yards.

Worked example: a 10 by 10 foot slab

10 × 10 feet, 4 inches thick

A 10 by 10 foot patio slab at 4 inches thick. Four inches is one-third of a foot. Run the numbers with the slab calculator to check your own size.

10 ft × 10 ft × 1/3 ft = 33.33 ft³, then 33.33 ÷ 27 = 1.23 yd³

Add 10% for waste and uneven ground: 33.33 × 1.10 = 36.67 cubic feet, which is 1.36 cubic yards. Order 1.36 cubic yards so you do not run short mid-pour.
