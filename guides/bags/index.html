Guide · Bags

How many bags of concrete do I need?

Count bags from your volume in cubic feet. One 80-pound bag fills 0.60 cubic feet, so 45 bags fill one cubic yard. A 10 by 10 foot slab at 4 inches thick needs 56 eighty-pound bags.

Bag yields and bags per cubic yard

Each bag size fills a set amount of concrete. Divide 27 cubic feet by that yield to get the bags per cubic yard.

Bag sizeFillsBags per cubic yard
40 lb0.30 ft³90
50 lb0.375 ft³72
60 lb0.45 ft³60
80 lb0.60 ft³45

Check the 80-pound row: 27 ÷ 0.60 = 45 bags. Check the 40-pound row: 27 ÷ 0.30 = 90 bags. Bigger bags mean fewer trips from the store, but each bag is heavier to lift.

How to count bags in three steps

  1. Work out the volume in cubic feet: length × width × thickness in feet. The slab calculator does this for slabs, and the cubic yard converter changes cubic feet into cubic yards.
  2. Divide the volume by the yield of one bag. For 80-pound bags, divide by 0.60.
  3. Round up. You cannot buy part of a bag, and running short mid-pour is worse than having one bag left over.

Example 1: a 10 × 10 foot slab, 4 inches thick

The slab is 10 × 10 × 1/3 feet = 33.33 cubic feet.

33.33 ft³ ÷ 0.60 ft³ per bag = 55.55, round up to 56 bags (80 lb)

Example 2: the same slab, with 10% waste

Add 10% for waste and uneven ground: 33.33 × 1.10 = 36.67 cubic feet.

36.67 ft³ ÷ 0.60 ft³ per bag = 61.11, round up to 62 bags (80 lb)

Example 3: a post hole, 12 inches wide and 4 feet deep

A round hole is a cylinder: π × radius² × depth. The radius is 6 inches, or 0.5 feet.

3.14 × 0.5² × 4 ft = 3.14 ft³, then 3.14 ÷ 0.60 = 5.24, round up to 6 bags (80 lb)

Bags or a ready-mix truck?

Around 1 cubic yard is the crossover point. Below that, bags are practical: a few bags, a wheelbarrow, and a mixer or a hoe. Above that, you are mixing and hauling 45 or more 80-pound bags, which takes hours of hard work. A ready-mix truck is the easier choice for bigger pours. Questions people ask

How many 80 lb bags of concrete are in a yard? +

45. One 80-pound bag fills 0.60 cubic feet, and 27 ÷ 0.60 = 45.

How many 60 lb bags of concrete are in a yard? +

60. One 60-pound bag fills 0.45 cubic feet, and 27 ÷ 0.45 = 60.

How many bags of concrete do I need for a 10 by 10 slab? +

56 eighty-pound bags at 4 inches thick. With 10% extra for waste, 62 bags.

Should I add extra bags for waste? +

Yes. Add 5 to 10% for a clean pour and 10 to 15% for rough or uneven ground. Always round up — a half bag short is a problem.

When should I order ready-mix instead of bags? +

Around 1 cubic yard. Above that, mixing dozens of bags by hand takes hours, and a ready-mix truck is the easier choice.
