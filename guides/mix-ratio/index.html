Concrete mix ratio: 1:2:3 explained

The standard concrete mix ratio is 1:2:3 — one part cement, two parts sand, and three parts aggregate by volume. That is about 17 percent cement, 33 percent sand, and 50 percent aggregate.

What each part does

Cement is the glue: mixed with water, it binds everything together and hardens as it cures. Sand fills the gaps between the stones and makes the mix smooth enough to place. Aggregate — gravel or crushed stone — gives the concrete its strength and bulk, and keeps it from shrinking and cracking as it hardens.

The three common ratios

RatioUse
1:2:3General slabs, paths, patios
1:1.5:3Higher strength: driveways, floors with heavy loads
1:3:6Lean mix: footings, fill, base layers

Parts are measured by volume, not by weight — use the same bucket or scoop for each part. The 1:1.5:3 mix has more cement than 1:2:3, so it is stronger. The 1:3:6 lean mix has less cement, so it is weaker and suits footings and fill, not slabs people walk or drive on.

Water: less is stronger

Water starts the curing reaction, but too much of it weakens the finished concrete and makes it crack more. Add only enough water to mix the concrete and place it. A stiffer mix is harder to work, but it cures stronger. If a mix feels too dry to place, add water a little at a time — never flood it.

Example: what goes into one cubic yard at 1:2:3

One cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. Split it by the mix shares — about 17% cement, 33% sand, and 50% aggregate.

Cement: 27 × 0.17 = 4.59 ft³
Sand: 27 × 0.33 = 8.91 ft³
Aggregate: 27 × 0.50 = 13.50 ft³

Check: 4.59 + 8.91 + 13.50 = 27.00 cubic feet. If you are pouring a slab, work out the volume first with the slab calculator, then split it the same way. A yard of mixed concrete weighs about 4,050 pounds — see the concrete weight guide before you haul it.

Questions people ask

What is the 1:2:3 concrete mix ratio? +

One part cement, two parts sand, and three parts aggregate by volume. It is the standard general-purpose mix for slabs, paths, and patios.

What concrete mix ratio should I use for a slab? +

1:2:3 works for most slabs. For a driveway or a floor that takes heavy loads, use the stronger 1:1.5:3 mix.

What is a 1:3:6 concrete mix used for? +

Lean mix for footings, fill, and base layers. It uses less cement, so it is weaker. Use it for footings and fill, not for slabs that take foot or vehicle traffic.

How much water do I add to concrete? +

Only enough to mix it and place it. Less water makes stronger concrete, but the mix gets stiffer and harder to work.

How much cement, sand, and aggregate are in one cubic yard at 1:2:3? +

About 5.94 cubic feet of cement, 8.91 cubic feet of sand, and 12.15 cubic feet of aggregate.
