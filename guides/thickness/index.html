Guide · Thickness

How thick should a concrete slab be?

Four inches works for patios, walkways, and shed floors. Driveways need 5 to 6 inches. Heavy loads like RVs and shop floors need 6 inches or more, usually with reinforcement.

4 inches: patios, walkways, and sheds

Four inches is the standard for slabs that only take foot traffic and light furniture: patios, walkways, shed floors, and residential floors. The load is light, so the slab does not need extra depth. One cubic yard at 4 inches thick covers 81 square feet.

5 to 6 inches: driveways

Driveways take cars and light trucks, so they need more depth. Use 5 inches for cars, and 6 inches if heavier vehicles will use the driveway. The extra inch spreads the load and helps the slab resist cracking under wheels.

6 inches or more: heavy loads

RV pads, shop floors, and slabs for heavy vehicles need 6 inches or more, usually with reinforcement such as rebar or mesh. The weight is far higher than a car, so the slab needs the extra depth to spread it. If you are unsure, ask a local contractor — the cost of a thicker slab is small next to the cost of replacing a cracked one.

How thickness changes your yardage

Thickness changes the order more than people expect. Two extra inches on a driveway is 50% more concrete. Work out the exact amount with the slab calculator.

Example: a 400 square foot driveway

At 4 inches thick, the slab is 400 × 1/3 feet deep. At 6 inches thick, it is 400 × 1/2 feet deep.

At 4 in: 400 × 1/3 ÷ 27 = 4.94 yd³
At 6 in: 400 × 1/2 ÷ 27 = 7.41 yd³

Going from 4 to 6 inches adds 2.47 cubic yards — 50% more concrete. Check: 7.41 ÷ 4.94 = 1.50. A yard of that concrete weighs about 4,050 pounds, so the thicker pour is also far heavier — see the concrete weight guide.

Does reinforcement change the volume?

No. Rebar or mesh helps hold cracks together, but it does not change how much concrete you order. The volume comes from length, width, and thickness only. Reinforcement is worth adding on driveways and heavy-load slabs, and the curing time guide explains when the slab is ready to use after the pour.

Questions people ask

How thick should a patio slab be? +

4 inches is enough for a patio. It only takes foot traffic and light furniture.

How thick should a driveway slab be? +

5 to 6 inches. Use 5 inches for cars and 6 inches if heavier vehicles will use it.

How thick should a slab be for an RV? +

6 inches or more, usually with reinforcement. An RV is far heavier than a car, so the slab needs the extra depth.

Can a concrete slab be too thick? +

A thicker slab is stronger, but it costs more concrete. Match the thickness to the load instead of pouring extra everywhere.

Does a thicker slab need reinforcement? +

Thicker slabs for heavy loads usually have rebar or mesh. Reinforcement holds cracks together — it does not change the amount of concrete you need.
