Guide · Weight

How much does a yard of concrete weigh?

One cubic yard of concrete weighs about 4,050 pounds — just over 2 tons. Standard concrete weighs 150 pounds per cubic foot, and a cubic yard holds 27 cubic feet.

The math: 150 pounds per cubic foot

Weight comes from density times volume. Standard concrete weighs 150 pounds per cubic foot, and one cubic yard holds 27 cubic feet: 150 × 27 = 4,050 pounds. In tons, that is 4,050 ÷ 2,000 = 2.03 tons — just over 2 tons.

Weight by mix

Not all concrete weighs the same. Steel reinforcement adds a little weight, and lightweight mixes use light aggregate that weighs much less.

Check the reinforced row: 156 × 27 = 4,212 pounds. Check the lightweight row: 110 × 27 = 2,970 pounds.

Weight by volume

VolumeWeight (standard concrete)
1 cubic foot150 lb

1 cubic meterAbout 5,297 lb

The cubic meter figure is 150 × 35.31 = 5,296.5 pounds, rounded to 5,297.

Example: a 10 × 10 foot slab, 4 inches thick

The slab is 33.33 cubic feet. At 150 pounds per cubic foot, that is a lot of weight sitting on the ground — check the size first with the slab calculator.

33.33 ft³ × 150 lb/ft³ = 5,000 lb, about 2.5 tons

Why the weight matters

First, forms and bracing must hold the weight while the concrete is wet — a yard of wet concrete pushes with over 2 tons. Second, transport has limits: a pickup or a small trailer cannot carry a full yard, so check the load rating before you haul. Third, the finished slab presses on the soil below, which is why the base and the slab thickness matter as much as the concrete itself.
